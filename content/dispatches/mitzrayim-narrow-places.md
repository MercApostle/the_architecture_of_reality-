The Hebrew name for Egypt, **Mitzrayim**, shares its root with *tsar* — narrow, constricted, distressed. In the ancient imagination, Egypt was not merely a place of slavery. It was a *condition*: the experience of being squeezed until there is no room left to choose.

## The narrow place in the brain

Modern neuroscience describes something remarkably similar. Under chronic threat, the brain shifts resources toward the amygdala and away from the prefrontal cortex. Attention narrows. Options collapse. You can still act — but only from a shrinking menu of reflexes.

> Mitzrayim is not where you are. It is how wide your world feels while you are there.

This is the biological signature of **Forcing Mode**: high stress, narrow aperture.

## The narrow place in the enterprise

Organisations have their own Mitzrayim. It shows up as a single founder through whom every decision must pass, or a culture where bad news travels slowly and good news is exaggerated. The bottleneck is rarely capacity. It is constriction.

## The Exodus protocol

Leaving the narrow place happens in stages:

1. **Name the constriction.** You cannot widen what you refuse to see.
2. **Regulate the vessel.** A dysregulated nervous system cannot perceive new options.
3. **Cross the threshold.** Take one action your threat-brain would never choose.

The question is not whether you will encounter narrow places. It is whether you will mistake them for home.
