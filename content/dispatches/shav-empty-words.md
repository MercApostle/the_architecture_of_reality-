"You shall not take the name… **in vain**." The Hebrew word is **Shav**: emptiness, falsehood, something without substance.

We usually read this as a rule about speech. But it is also an engineering principle: **a name carries weight; attach it to nothing and the whole system weakens.**

## Empty words as energy leaks

Every time a commitment is made and not kept, three things happen:

1. Trust erodes — quietly, but measurably.
2. Coordination costs rise, because others must now build in redundancy.
3. The speaker's own internal signal weakens. Saying and doing drift apart.

<!-- audit-cta -->

## Shav in the operating system

In organisations, Shav appears as mission statements nobody believes, roadmaps that are never followed, and meetings that end without decisions. Each one teaches the system that words are optional.

## Restoring substance

The fix is not more words. It is fewer, heavier ones:

- Make fewer promises.
- Keep every promise you make — exactly as stated.
- Retire statements the organisation no longer lives.

When words become load-bearing again, the whole architecture strengthens.
